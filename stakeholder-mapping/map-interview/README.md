# Map interview

Interview stakeholders in your own area and let them draw on a map where and why the mangrove changed. This is the
method behind the Stakeholders page of the Sisal Mangrove Monitor.

## What the method is

A semi-structured interview in four parts: general questions, mangrove change and its drivers, communication, and the
present and future. Question 5 is the mapping step: the stakeholder draws the areas that changed most on a map and
explains what caused each change. Interviews are recorded and transcribed. Each stakeholder type also gets a short
module with its own questions (developers, eco-tourism, fishers, government, residents, researchers).

In Sisal we interviewed seven stakeholders in September and October 2026. Questions 2 to 5 (change, causes, sudden
events and the map) are the basis of the three themes on the Stakeholders page. The next step, turning the interviews
into a table, is in [interview-to-table](../interview-to-table/).

## What is in this folder

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `interview_guide_en.pdf`, `interview_guide_es.pdf` | The interview guide in English and Spanish, with the questions and the modules per stakeholder type. |
| `consent_form_en.pdf`, `consent_form_es.pdf` | The consent form in English and Spanish: recording, use of answers and drawings, and data storage. |
| `sisal_base_map.pdf` | The base map for question 5: Sentinel-2 image of Sisal (January–May 2024), A3 landscape, black and white, with scale bar, north arrow, a light longitude/latitude grid and space at the side to number the areas. |

The forms have blank name, signature and date lines, and a placeholder for the contact details of the research team and
the supervisor: fill these in before you print.

## What you need

- The interview guide and consent form (this folder).
- A printed map of your area for question 5. For another area, print one, or export one with the Sentinel-2 script from
  the Ecosystem method.
- A recorder, and an interpreter if you do not speak the local language.

## Steps

1. Print a map of your area for the drawing question, or export one with the Sentinel-2 script from the Ecosystem method.
2. Read the consent form together and ask for the three consents (recording, use of answers and drawings, data
   storage). Give the stakeholder a code: type + number, for example NGO1.
3. Ask the general questions, then the questions on change and drivers, and add the module for this stakeholder type.
4. At question 5, let the stakeholder draw and number each area. Ask when it changed and why, per area.
5. Transcribe the recording, photograph the map, and store names and recordings separately from the codes.

## Notes

- Use the code, never the name, in everything you write down after the interview.
- Areas drawn by hand show where people meant, not exact boundaries. Periods come from memory.
- Templates are under CC BY 4.0, code under MIT.

Base map of the example: Sentinel-2 (Copernicus), January–May 2024, Sisal, Yucatán, Mexico.
